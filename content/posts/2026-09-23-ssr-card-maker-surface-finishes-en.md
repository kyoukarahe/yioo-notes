Under `Card Details → Surface Finish`, choose metallic reflections, film gloss and patterns, and UV coatings. While the Foil menu controls the flow of light and fine surface textures, this menu controls the appearance of metal, film, and coating materials.

[Open SSR Card Maker](https://yioo.link/tools/en/image/ssr-card-maker/) · [Getting started](/notes/2026-09-23-ssr-card-maker-guide-en/)

Select a comparison image to enlarge it. On mobile, swipe horizontally within a table if it is wider than the screen.

In these tables, **Before means the card before the finish being compared was applied**. The card already contains the uploaded artwork, frame, and text. The examples use the same character and background, custom frame, `Karahe` title, and bottom caption. Card stock, white underprint, special ink, emboss/deboss, and the Foil menu’s reflections and textures were disabled. Separate hologram materials for the frame and text were also disabled.

Unless stated otherwise, single-finish examples use **100% strength, all four application areas selected, Daylight, and the same Recommended angle**. Strength was increased to make differences visible; adjust it to suit your own artwork. Enlarged comparisons crop the same area on both sides, with no color, brightness, or contrast correction.

## How the three groups work together

- **Hologram Materials**: chooses a material’s reflective character, such as gold, silver, colored, rainbow, or pearlescent.
- **Film Finish**: changes the surface appearance with gloss or matte finishes, or adds lines, grids, and light patterns.
- **UV Coating**: adds gloss and texture across the surface or in a specific pattern. Here, UV is the name of the coating category; its effects are visible in Daylight too.

Select **one option within each group**. Gloss and Matte films cannot be selected together, but options from different groups, such as Matte film and Spot UV, can be combined. To disable one, select `None` or use that option’s `Remove` button.

## What to keep in mind when choosing

Some finishes have similar names or appearances. Start with one, adjust its strength and application areas, then add another group to make comparisons easier. On artwork that is already bright or strongly colored, patterns and reflection positions may be easier to see than subtle color shifts.

This guide describes digital finishes shown by the tool. Check a print shop’s specifications and samples for actual film thickness, texture, scratch resistance, color reproduction, and production results.
