Under `Card Details → Light, Environment & Ink`, choose **the lighting environment in which you view the card** and **the ink that responds to it**. See how the card changes in Daylight, Ultraviolet, and Dark room environments. You can also keep foil reflection patterns and colors enabled alongside them.

[Open SSR Card Maker](https://yioo.link/tools/en/image/ssr-card-maker/) · [Getting started](/notes/2026-09-23-ssr-card-maker-guide-en/)

Select a comparison image to enlarge it. On mobile, swipe horizontally within a table if it is wider than the screen.

The examples use the same character and background, custom frame, `Karahe` title, and bottom caption. For comparison, card stock, white underprint, emboss/deboss, surface finishes, and separate foil reflections and textures were disabled. Separate hologram materials for the frame and text were also disabled.

In these tables, **Before means the card before the effect was applied**. Single-ink examples use 100% strength, all Character, Background, Frame, and Text areas selected, and the same Recommended angle. Strength was increased to show differences; adjust it to your own artwork. Exceptions are noted in the tables. No color, brightness, or contrast correction was applied.

## Choose the lighting environment first

You can select `Daylight`, `Ultraviolet`, or `Dark room` without choosing an ink. In the current tool, switching to Ultraviolet or Dark room darkens a card even without ink. **Changing lighting and ink at the same time makes it hard to tell what caused the difference. Choose the lighting first, then enable the ink in that same environment.**

## Special ink

Choose **one ink at a time**. You can combine it with effects from other menus, such as foil or film.

Fluorescent and Phosphorescent inks currently **appear in the list only when both character and background images have been uploaded separately**. Even if your artwork already contains a background, each of the two upload slots must contain a file. This guide’s project has both.

Only the application area changes; UV strength and Fluorescent ink strength both stay at 100%. Excluding the background and frame makes the character’s glow easier to distinguish. Unselected areas may still retain **darkening caused by the lighting environment**.

`Strength` controls how prominently the ink effect appears. If applying 100% to all areas changes the artwork’s colors too much, lower the strength or narrow the application area. `Show areas`, which identifies coverage, and `View effect only`, which shows the material response without artwork, are different functions. Both are for inspection while editing.

## How to move the light

Move the card preview in the editor to inspect reflections at different angles. Under View tools, `Pointer response` determines whether the card responds directly or follows the pointer smoothly. `Front-facing light sweep` keeps the card facing forward while light sweeps across it.

**Playback in View tools and the animation you export are selected separately.** For PNG, set the angle or the front-facing light-sweep position under Export. For GIF and WebP, choose Card rotation or the light sweep as the animation to save. The ink’s `Elapsed time` is separate from the animation’s Total duration.

## When changes are hard to see

- **Fluorescent or Phosphorescent is missing**: check that both character and background files have been uploaded.
- **Fluorescent ink looks almost unchanged in Daylight**: switch to Ultraviolet and compare it with no ink under the same lighting.
- **Phosphorescent ink stays dark**: check Dark room, Stored charge, Elapsed time, and the application areas.
- **Returning to Daylight does not restore the initial appearance**: check for remaining environment values. Selecting a mode does not reset every slider.
- **Text and frame change differently from the artwork**: differences depend on each area’s original color, transparency, and selected application areas.

This tool provides a digital impression of lighting and ink. Check a print shop’s specifications and samples for actual fluorescent colors, glow duration, and color-shifting performance.
